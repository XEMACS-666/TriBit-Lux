Follow the signal.

01 · LDR and sensitivity adjustment

Light lowers the LDR’s resistance. In the saved V2.1 divider, the LDR is above the sensor node and RV1 provides the adjustable path to ground, so the analog voltage rises as the LDR resistance falls. Sensitivity depends on the potentiometer setting and the LDR’s response.

VOUT ≈ 2.5 × Rlower / (RLDR + Rlower)

J1 pin 1 is 5 V input, pin 2 is ground, and pin 3 is the analog sensor output. That header does not expose the three-bit code.

02 · TL431 reference and resistor ladder

The TL431 supplies an approximately 2.5 V sensor/reference rail. Eight equal 10 kΩ resistors divide it into eight ideal bands. Seven nonzero taps set the transitions; the comparator and logic supply remains 5 V.

Tₖ = k × VREF / 8, for k = 1…7

Nominal transitions: 0.3125, 0.6250, 0.9375, 1.2500, 1.5625, 1.8750 and 2.1875 V. V2.0 instead uses a 3.3 V reference and 0.4125 V steps.

03 · LM339 comparator bank and LED buffer

Two quad LM339s provide eight comparator channels: seven compare the sensor with ladder taps, and the eighth uses the ground reference for the base condition. The nonzero-threshold channels place the sensor on their non-inverting inputs. Below a threshold, the output sinks LOW; above it, the pull-up can bring the output HIGH.

The non-inverting 74HCT244 buffers these seven signals to drive the cumulative LEDs through series resistors. The lab shows the ideal crossed-threshold states; it does not simulate propagation delays, analog noise or LED loading.

04 · The active-LOW priority encoder

The 74LS148 uses active-LOW priority inputs and inverted binary outputs. The saved netlist connects ascending thresholds to I7 through I1. As the sensor rises, those inputs release in sequence; the highest-priority remaining LOW input selects the displayed level. The ground-reference comparator feeds I0.

S0, S1 and S2 connect directly to the decoder’s A, B and C inputs. The lab presents the resulting level code, from 000 to 111. At exact equality, this educational model assigns the higher band; real comparators have offset and noise.

05 · Decoder and seven-segment display

The 74LS48 converts the three encoder bits to segment signals. Its D input is grounded, restricting the intended range to 0–7. The common-cathode display and its series resistors are shown in the original schematic.

The browser reproduces digit shapes, not electrical drive currents. V1.1 still used the Nano for display decoding; V1.2 introduced the hardware decoder.

06 · Design evidence and open checks

The V2.1 README records zero DRC errors, zero unconnected items and zero schematic-parity issues, with 57 warnings remaining at the 30 October 2026 checkpoint. These are design checks, not results from fabricated hardware.

Fresh Gerber export, supplier DFM review, U7 placement orientation, physical fit and indicator-to-LDR optical coupling remain to be checked. V2.2 is reserved for the fabricated result.
